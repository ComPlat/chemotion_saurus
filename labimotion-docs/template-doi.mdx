Any generic template can be given its own [DataCite](https://datacite.org/) **DOI**, so that the
template itself — not just the data recorded with it — becomes a citable object. This applies equally
to **element**, **segment** and **dataset** templates: each Designer lists its templates with a
**DOI Info.** column, and every row carries the same **DOI** button that opens the same dialog.

From that dialog, the designer fills in the citation metadata, **reserves** a DOI, and later **releases** it.
Releasing publishes the template as its next major version to the **Template Hub**, where anyone can
look it up, read its metadata and see what the template actually looks like.

Template DOIs are not registered on their own: they go through the **Chemotion Repository**'s DOI
registration, under the same `10.14272` prefix as the collection, reaction, sample and analysis DOIs
the Repository mints for research data. [DOI Issues](/docs/repo/doi) explains how that registry works
and how its DOIs are structured.

## Before you start

:::info[Registration runs through the Chemotion Repository]

The DOI itself is minted by the **Chemotion Repository**, which holds the DataCite prefix and does
the registering. The Designer collects the metadata and drives the workflow, but the identifier and
the registry entry are the Repository's.

Designers who would like their templates registered can write to
**[chemotion-labimotion@lists.kit.edu](mailto:chemotion-labimotion@lists.kit.edu)**. The LabIMotion
admins will help with setup and guide them through it.

:::

Also required:

- a template that is ready to be published — an element, a segment or a dataset template;
- the metadata to be registered with the DOI: a **title**, a **description**, at least one
  **author**, and a **license**.

## 1. Open the Designer

From the user menu at the top-right of the navigation bar (showing the user's name), choose
**Generic Designer**, then open the Designer for the kind of template to be published —
**Elements**, **Segments** or **Datasets**. Each opens a grid listing every template of that kind.

The grid's last column, **DOI Info.**, carries a **DOI** button on each row. That button is the entry
point to the DOI workflow for that template, and its color encodes the state: plain when no DOI is
reserved, blue once one is reserved, green once it has been released.

## 2. Open the DOI dialog

In the template's row, press **DOI**. A dialog opens, titled for that template, with its current
status shown next to the title — **Not reserved**, **Reserved** or **Released**, each carrying the DOI
version it refers to.

The dialog has three parts:

- **Metadata** — the *Title*, *Description*, *Authors*, optional *Contributors* and *References*, and
  the *License* that will be registered with the DOI.
- **Versions** — the *Released DOI version*, the *Next DOI version*, and the *Current template
  version*.
- **Actions** — *Save metadata*, *Reserve DOI*, *Release DOI*, *Preview metadata XML* and *Close*.

:::tip[DOIs are versioned — one per major template version]

A DOI is pinned to a **major** template version, not to every edit. Minor revisions keep sharing the
DOI they were released under; crossing a major boundary is what earns a new one.

| Template version | DOI version |
| --- | --- |
| 0.1 – 1.0 | 1 |
| 1.1 – 2.0 | 2 |
| 2.1 – 3.0 | 3 |

The **last path segment of the DOI suffix is the DOI version** — a suffix ending `…/1` is DOI
version 1. So one DOI covers a *range* of template versions, and a reader can tell which range from
the DOI alone.

A released DOI is frozen: its metadata form becomes read-only. The form unlocks — and **Reserve DOI**
becomes **Reserve new version** — only once the template has moved on to a new major version. The
**Versions** block shows where the template stands: which DOI version is released, which one is next,
and where the template version currently sits.

:::

## 3. Fill in the metadata

- **Title** — a human-readable name for the template.
- **Description** — what the template captures, and which standard or protocol it follows.
- **Authors** — the designer can be added with **Add me**, or people can be pulled in with
  **Add from my collaborators…**. Each author row carries a given name, family name, ORCID and
  affiliation. At least one author is required. Author details are drawn from the account and its
  [collaborators](/docs/repo/settings-preparation), so the ORCID and affiliations should be kept up
  to date.
- **Contributors** and **References** — optional.
- **License** — choose the reuse license: **CC BY-SA**, **CC BY** (the default), **CC0** or
  **No License**.

Press **Save metadata** to store what has been entered. The dialog can be reopened to revise the
metadata as often as needed *before* the DOI is released.

## 4. Reserve the DOI

Press **Reserve DOI**. This creates and reserves the DOI **locally**, with its **final, deterministic
suffix** — the identifier is settled from this moment on, so it can already be quoted in a manuscript
or a preprint, and it is the same identifier that will later be minted.

Nothing has reached DataCite yet. Reserving allocates the identifier and makes it possible to preview
the record; the external registry is contacted only at release.

Once a DOI is reserved, **Release DOI** and **Preview metadata XML** become available — the latter
shows the exact DataCite record that will be published.

:::caution[Reserving cannot be undone]

*Reserve DOI* is not a preview or a draft that can be backed out of — it writes a real local record and
fixes the exact suffix that will later be minted. **There is no unreserve action.** The only thing
reserving does *not* do is contact the external registry.

:::

## 5. Release the DOI

When the metadata is final, press **Release DOI**. This is the step that reaches the outside world:

- it creates the record at **DataCite**, so the DOI resolves and is **findable**;
- it publishes the template as its next major version to the **Template Hub**, where the released
  version and its DOI now appear.

:::danger[Releasing a DOI is permanent]

The registry is contacted exactly once, at release — and a released DOI **cannot be deleted**. The
metadata or the target of a released DOI can be updated later, but it cannot be un-published. Release
only when the template and its metadata are genuinely final.

:::

## 6. Find the published template in the Template Hub

The Template Hub's **Find a Template** view is how a reader gets from a DOI to the thing it
identifies. It asks for three things in order, each unlocking the next:

1. **Template type** — Generic Element, Generic Segment or Generic Dataset
2. **Template name / label** — listed by *label*, so look for the human-readable name
3. **Release version** — shown as `v1.0 (released …)`, defaulting to the newest

It then renders a card with the template's metadata — type, name, label, description, prefix,
version, released date, identifier and **DOI**, with *Copy DOI* and a *Metadata XML* download of the
DataCite record — followed by **Example**: the template's real form, rendered live and interactively,
layer cards and all.

That view is also the DOI's **landing page**. A released DOI is registered against a deep link into
this finder, with the type, template and version pre-selected, so following the DOI takes a reader
straight to the publisher metadata *and* to a working preview of the template — rather than to a bare
record.

From here, any Designer on another instance can synchronize the template into their own instance — see
[Template Sync & Publish](template-sync-and-publish).

## The DOI lifecycle at a glance

Three states, and the transitions between them run one way only.

| State | What exists | Reached by | Public? | Reversible? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Not reserved** | Citation metadata only, if any has been filled in. No identifier allocated. | — | No | Metadata can be edited and saved again |
| **Reserved** | A **local DOI record** with the **final, deterministic suffix**. The DOI can already be quoted in a manuscript or preprint. **DataCite has not been contacted.** | **Reserve DOI** | No — it does not resolve yet | **No — there is no unreserve** |
| **Released** | The record is created at **DataCite**. The DOI resolves, is findable and is citable, and the template version is published to the Template Hub. | **Release DOI** | Yes | **No — a released DOI is permanent** |

Because the suffix is settled at reserve time, the identifier cited before publication is the same
one that ends up in DataCite afterwards.

## Questions and support

Any question about this workflow — the metadata, the DOI, versioning, or finding a published
template — reaches the LabIMotion team at
**[chemotion-labimotion@lists.kit.edu](mailto:chemotion-labimotion@lists.kit.edu)**. An issue can also be
[filed](https://github.com/LabIMotion/labimotion) or a thread started on the
[discussion board](https://github.com/LabIMotion/labimotion/discussions).

:::tip

Neither reserving nor releasing can be undone, so questions should be asked *before* pressing them, not after.

:::
